History of Swiss Food

Cheese Fondue

Cheese fondue dates back to the 18th century in the Swiss Alps, where it was created by farmers during harsh winters. With limited food available, they combined hardened cheese, stale bread, and wine to make a warm and filling meal. Over time, fondue became a symbol of Swiss culture and togetherness, especially after being promoted as a national dish in the 1930s.


Raclette

Raclette originated in the Valais region of Switzerland, where shepherds would melt cheese over an open fire and scrape it onto potatoes. The name “raclette” comes from the French word meaning “to scrape.” It gradually became a popular social dish, often enjoyed during gatherings and special occasions.


Zürcher Geschnetzeltes

Zürcher geschnetzeltes is a traditional dish from Zurich that dates back to the 19th century. It is made with sliced veal cooked in a creamy white wine sauce, often combined with mushrooms. This dish became one of the most famous specialties of Zurich and is commonly served with rösti.


Rösti

Rösti began as a simple farmer’s breakfast in the canton of Bern, made from grated potatoes fried until crispy. Originally eaten in the mornings, it later spread throughout Switzerland and is now served as a side dish for various meals. It also became a cultural symbol, representing regional differences within the country.


Bircher Müesli

Bircher müesli was invented around 1900 by Swiss doctor Maximilian Bircher-Brenner. He developed it as a healthy dish for his patients, combining oats, fruits, and milk. Its nutritional benefits made it popular, and it eventually became a common breakfast not only in Switzerland but around the world.
